# Educational Philosophy

For v5, [Product Bible v5](product-bible-v5.md) is the source of truth for product structure and priorities. This document records the educational convictions that support it.

Puritan Parser exists to help people become independent readers of Scripture in Biblical Greek and Hebrew.

It is a reading companion, not a first-year language course. It assumes many users have already completed introductory Greek or Hebrew study and need review, reteaching, recognition practice, and reading preparation. The goal is not to keep users inside the app. The goal is to help them read Scripture with less dependence on the app over time.

## Read, Learn, and Reference

Puritan Parser should keep three related modes distinct:

- Read: help me read the biblical text.
- Learn: help me become a better reader tomorrow.
- Reference: help me understand a question I have right now.

Reference may be expansive. Learn should remain focused, lightweight, and fluency-oriented. Read should always keep the biblical text central.

## Learn

Learn is not a gamified habit system. It should not use streak pressure, XP, artificial badges, guilt mechanics, or other engagement patterns that compete with Scripture for attention.

Learn should support:

- Vocabulary
- Paradigms and recognition practice
- Reading Readiness

It should invite the learner forward without forcing a single path.

## Vocabulary

Puritan Parser should have one vocabulary collection and one long-term review system. Study paths determine what new words enter that review system; they do not create separate decks.

Supported study paths should eventually include:

- By frequency
- By book
- By book and frequency
- By chapter
- By chapter and frequency

Examples:

- All Greek words 10+
- All words in Romans
- All 5+ words in Romans
- All 5+ words in Romans 8

## Known Vocabulary

A word should not count as Known merely because it has been introduced. Known should mean the user has demonstrated reliable recognition over time.

Reading Readiness should count Known words, not merely introduced words.

Suggested vocabulary states:

- Not Learned
- Learning
- Known

## Reading Readiness

Reading Readiness should answer: what stands between me and reading this book or chapter comfortably?

Initial readiness should be objective. Use Known Vocabulary and Remaining Words. Do not invent subjective labels like comfortable or independent until the app has enough real data to support them. When thresholds are unknown, present objective information instead of subjective interpretation.

Reading Readiness and By Book study should share the same underlying Book Progress page.

## Book and Chapter Study

Study should match the user's reading scope. If the user wants to read Romans, they should be able to study Romans vocabulary. If the user wants to read Romans 8, they should be able to study Romans 8 vocabulary.

Frequency milestones within books and chapters are meaningful:

- All 25+ words in Romans
- All 10+ words in Romans
- All 5+ words in Romans
- All 5+ words in Romans 8

Milestones should represent real reading ability, not app activity.

## Word Page Learning Status

Word Pages should eventually show the user's relationship to a word:

- Not Learned
- Learning
- Known

If a word is Not Learned, the Word Page may quietly offer Learn This Word. This should be optional. The app should invite, not force.

## Paradigm and Recognition Practice

Recognition is the main goal. Production is secondary and optional. Typing Greek or Hebrew should not be the default workflow.

Practice should mirror the skill being developed: recognizing forms while reading. Prefer feature-by-feature recognition over one giant parsing answer.

Greek verb recognition may include:

- Tense or aspect
- Voice
- Mood
- Person
- Number

Greek noun recognition may include:

- Case
- Number
- Gender

Hebrew verb recognition may include:

- Stem
- Conjugation
- Person
- Gender
- Number
- Suffix when present

Hebrew noun recognition may include:

- Gender
- Number
- State
- Suffix when present

## Paradigm Curriculum

Learn assumes prior exposure. It is review and reteaching, not first exposure. Reference may contain fuller explanations. Learn should contain minimal explanation and direct users to Reference when deeper understanding is needed.

Greek and Hebrew should share the same recognition philosophy but not identical curricula.

Greek practice should be verb-heavy. Treat participles and infinitives as part of the Greek verbal system because that is how readers often experience them.

Greek verbs should include:

- Indicative forms
- Subjunctive
- Imperative
- Optative
- Participles
- Infinitives
- Contract verbs
- mi verbs
- Principal parts
- Irregular verbs

Other Greek parts of speech are useful but secondary:

- Nouns
- Pronouns
- Articles
- Adjectives

Hebrew practice should also be verb-heavy. Hebrew verbs should include:

- Stems
- Conjugations
- Weak verbs
- Wayyiqtol
- Weqatal
- Imperatives
- Participles
- Infinitives
- Verb suffixes

Suffix recognition should be incorporated into Hebrew verb learning where appropriate.

Other Hebrew topics remain important but secondary:

- Nouns
- Construct state
- Pronominal suffixes
- Adjectives
- Pronouns
- Prepositions

## Feedback and Correction

Incorrect answers should become learning opportunities. Corrective feedback should be available, not obligatory. Feedback should be brief and unobtrusive, and the learner should always be able to continue quickly.

Eventually, explanation level may be user-configurable:

- Minimal
- Standard
- Detailed

## Recommendations

Recommendations should be evidence-based, infrequent, and easy to dismiss. Puritan Parser should recommend, not dictate.

If a learner repeatedly misses a concept, the app may gently suggest a relevant review or Reference page.

## Review Backlog

The app should never punish absence.

If the user has many reviews available, it may offer:

- Continue Review: use the normal review schedule.
- Catch Up Review: prioritize the most important overdue reviews first.

Catch Up Review should appear only when useful, such as after several days away and a sufficiently large backlog.

## Progress and Statistics

Progress should answer:

- What can I read?
- How are my languages improving?
- What should I study next?
- How has my reading ability changed over time?

Statistics may answer:

- How many reviews have I completed?
- How many cards have I studied?
- How has my usage changed over time?

Progress should be prominent. Statistics can be more hidden or secondary.

Progress measures ability. Statistics satisfy curiosity.

## Reference

Reference can be expansive. Quick-reference material should appear first. More niche or advanced material should remain available but should not clog the first screen.

Reference should be organized by usefulness while reading, not merely by grammar textbook order.

Depth should be unlimited. Complexity should not be.
