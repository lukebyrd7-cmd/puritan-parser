## Resolution rules

Source strings are split conservatively on explicit semicolon-style separators and on the existing source comma convention, trimmed, and deduplicated case-insensitively while preserving first spelling and source order. Standard, additive personal, and replacement personal modes produce one effective presentation. Occurrence glosses never enter this resolver.

The resolver performs no writes. Personal edits change only `pp_personal_glosses`; they do not touch scheduling, mastery, Needs attention, Reader locations, or canonical source records.

## Learner-facing presentation cleanup

The shared presentation resolver removes an article-prefixed sense only when the same record also contains the exact bare sense, compared case-insensitively. Thus `a family; family; kind` becomes `family; kind`, while a fixed phrase with no bare companion remains unchanged. The compact canonical sources contain 508 affected Hebrew records and no affected Greek records. Raw source strings are preserved.

The Strong's Hebrew source contains three multi-word sense fragments whose final lowercase `i` is a truncated `i.e.` notation fragment: source IDs `120` (`ruddy i`), `3433` (`' i`), and `3433+` (`' i`). The presentation resolver removes terminal lowercase ` i` only for multi-token senses explicitly attributed to Strong's Hebrew Dictionary. It does not remove standalone `i`, uppercase `I`, or Roman-numeral content such as `Jeroboam I`. The raw canonical source and attribution remain unchanged.

`hb-28058` (`lemma: i`, frequency 1) is unrelated to this notation rule. It remains unsupported and visible to the audit, and remains ineligible for learner cards pending a trusted source-backed resolution.

## Hebrew Reader lexical identity

The Hebrew interlinear attachment previously retained token-level gloss metadata but discarded its ordered segment records. Word Details then derived the primary part of speech from the beginning of whole-token morphology, so attached `HR`, `HC`, or `HT` components could appear as the lexical identity of a noun, verb, adjective, or pronoun.

The Reader now preserves those trusted segment records. Interlinear display already has them attached; a Standard-mode Word Details selection loads only its requested interlinear chapter and matches the stable token ID before resolving the selected word. The stable numeric token lemma continues to select the canonical vocabulary record, frequency, gloss, and learning ID. The selected non-suffix lexical-content segment supplies only the readable lexical lemma and lexical part of speech. Prefix and suffix segments remain subordinate Word structure data, and the complete token morphology remains occurrence parsing.

The bounded corpus audit covers 929 Hebrew interlinear chapter files and 306,785 tokens. Of 145,993 multi-segment tokens, 103,863 have a first structural segment part of speech different from the resolved lexical part of speech, across 143 structural patterns; all resolve to a trusted lexical segment. Greek has no equivalent segmented-token path and retained its existing identity behavior.
