# v1.8.2 Reader details audit

## Interaction and lifecycle

Reader tokens share one Greek/Hebrew click path: `renderReaderTokens` emits language-explicit buttons, `openReaderTokenPopup` resolves the selected occurrence, and `renderReaderWordPopup` mounts either the desktop side panel or mobile overlay. The side panel scrolls independently; the overlay is a modal sheet. Escape and the overlay close control dismiss details and restore focus to the selected token. A temporary mobile history entry makes browser Back dismiss the sheet before leaving Reader. Opening a Word Page preserves the Reader location and uses the existing view route.

Reader controls are rewired after each render, but existing document, scroll, resize, and history listeners are removed before being added. Canonical vocabulary records are indexed once during Reader initialization and reused for word selections. Gloss sources, chapter data, interlinear data, translations, manifests, and search indexes retain their existing caches. Opening read-only details performs no persistence write.

## Display-data boundaries

- Occurrence form, morphology, qere/ketiv, source lemma, and token identity come from the selected chapter token.
- Passage reference comes from the rendered token's book, chapter, and verse attributes.
- Canonical lexical glosses come from `data/glosses/<language>-glosses.json` and canonical vocabulary records. Occurrence glosses remain contextual aids and are only a final lexical fallback.
- Greek canonical lemma is the token lemma. Hebrew display lemma/root is resolved from existing lexical-form, Hebrew-lemma, root, and vocabulary fields; numeric Strong's identifiers are never displayed as headwords.
- Part of speech and morphology labels use trusted parse codes through the existing parser and Reader field decoders.
- Hebrew prefixes come from slash-delimited OSHB source lemmas; suffix features come from OSHB morphology. No segmentation is inferred from spelling.
- Total frequency is the aggregate canonical vocabulary frequency, with the Reader search index as a fallback.
- Stable vocabulary identity is `VocabularyLearning.lemmaId(entry)` and is shared by learning, Needs attention, and Custom Deck membership.

## Morphology finding

Compact Greek participles such as `V-PAP-NSM` previously read number from the end of `NSM`, so case and gender could appear while singular/plural was omitted. The Reader now treats the participle tail as case-number-gender, omits person, and keeps infinitives free of nominal/person fields. Hebrew verb and nominal summaries continue to expose only decoded stem, conjugation, person, gender, number, state, prefix, and suffix fields present in OSHB data.

## Persistence and import/export

`pp_reader_location` now has schema version 2 with `activeLanguage` and independent `locations.greek` and `locations.hebrew` records. Each location retains book, chapter, verse, mode, anchor offset, scroll positions, and an update timestamp. Legacy single-language records migrate in memory and are written nondestructively on the next Reader preference/location write. A legacy one-language import merges into that language and preserves the other valid location; a version-2 import carries both. Adaptive Reader settings remain in `pp_reader_adaptive_settings`.

## Gloss and first-use behavior

`GlossModel.presentLexicalGlosses` performs non-destructive render-time splitting, deduplication, semicolon presentation, compact primary grouping, and missing-gloss labeling. Reader shows up to three primary senses and discloses the remainder. Revealed practice cards show ordinary concise senses immediately; only unusually long text receives a clamped full-list disclosure. English-first cards expose only the English prompt before Reveal.

First-use guidance is computed independently per language from zero Known, zero Learning/Reviewing, zero due reviews, and no active session. The starter action filters through the centralized card validator and saves a finite, reading-direction session containing up to the five highest-frequency studyable New records. Reveal and exit do not introduce a word; the first confidence submission creates its normal learning record. An unfinished starter session is resumed rather than duplicated.
