# v1.9.5 — Canonical Vocabulary Forms & Hebrew Morphology

## Problem and root cause

Reader occurrences and vocabulary identities were correctly distinguished by stable ID, but Hebrew vocabulary rows in `vocab_all.json` contain numeric OSHB/Strong identities and occurrence forms rather than a canonical headword field. Lemma grouping selected the highest-frequency row as its representative. The shared practice validator then searched `representativeForm`, `word`, and the occurrence `forms` collection when no Hebrew-script lemma was present. Consequently the stable identity remained correct while the learner-facing card front could become an article-bearing noun, construct, plural, suffixed form, wayyiqtol, or other frequent occurrence.

The failure was therefore a combination of:

- a missing canonical-form field in the generated Hebrew vocabulary rows;
- a surface-form fallback in the shared card validator;
- UI helpers that permitted `word` as a final headword fallback;
- incomplete, duplicated learner-facing Hebrew stem-code expansion in Parser and Reader.

Reader-to-deck actions and Custom Deck storage already retained stable vocabulary IDs. They did not create new lexical identities, but an old embedded occurrence display could win later because the render path lacked an authoritative stable-ID lookup.

## Reader form versus lexical form

Reader continues to render the exact biblical occurrence. Its token, segmentation, prefix, suffix, and occurrence morphology are not rewritten.

Vocabulary display now follows one invariant:

```text
stable vocabulary ID → canonical-form map → learner-facing lexical form
```

`src/models/canonical-forms.js` owns this resolution. `src/core/study-entries.js`, the shared practice validator, legacy flashcards, Learn, Daily Practice, English-first reveal, starter sessions, Custom Decks, Needs attention, Search-backed study entries, and vocabulary lists consume the same resolved field. The resolver never uses `word`, `representativeForm`, or `forms` as a card-front fallback. A missing mapped numeric identity fails card eligibility instead of displaying a corpus occurrence.

## Sources and canonical-form rules

The compact runtime overlay is `data/lexical/canonical-forms.json`.

- Greek uses the established MorphGNT SBLGNT canonical lemma for all 5,478 identities.
- Numeric Hebrew OSHB/Strong identities use the canonical Hebrew lemma in the approved public-domain Open Scriptures Strong’s source pinned in `data/metadata/open-scriptures-strongs-source.json`.
- Existing Hebrew-script lexical identities retain their established lexical form.
- Bound source identities `l`, `b`, `m`, `k`, and `i` resolve to the existing function-word model for לְ, בְּ, מִן, כְּ, and הֲ.
- `vocab_all.json` and every biblical corpus file remain unchanged.

The overlay is loaded once with vocabulary data and copied onto runtime entries as `canonicalForm`/`lexicalForm`. Resolution is then an indexed map lookup; no morphology is reconstructed while rendering a card. The overlay is an offline seed in `sw.js`.

## Hebrew nouns, adjectives, clitics, and function words

Nouns and adjectives use the source headword, not a frequent construct, plural, dual, article-bearing, prefixed, or pronominally suffixed occurrence. Attached ו, ה, ב, ל, כ, and מ remain occurrence morphology in Reader. Their function-word identities remain separate stable vocabulary identities where already modeled. This release corrects display resolution and does not broaden ordinary-practice eligibility.

Proper names retain their own stable IDs and source headwords. Homonymous stable IDs remain separate even when their canonical Hebrew spelling is the same.

## Hebrew verbs and scholarly exception policy

The final citation audit classifies every one of the 1,603 stable identities that has a verb row. Gesenius-Kautzsch-Cowley §§39 and 72–73 supply the controlling rule: Qal perfect 3ms is normally the lexical ground form, while the relevant middle-weak classes use a lexicon ground form that retains the medial weak radical rather than displaying the two-consonant perfect.

| Classification | Count | Rule |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `EXACT_QAL_PERFECT_3MS_CORPUS` | 475 | OSHB supplies an exact Qal perfect 3ms and the Open Scriptures Lexical Index independently confirms the displayed identity as a verb. |
| `SOURCE_SUPPORTED_QAL_PERFECT_3MS` | 548 | Qal is corpus-supported; Strong’s and the Open Scriptures Lexical Index agree on the verb headword, which follows the normal GKC ground-form rule, but that exact inflection is absent from OSHB. |
| `LEGITIMATE_NON_QAL_EXCEPTION` | 580 | A middle-weak ground form, derived-stem sense, corpus-without-Qal identity, non-verbal Strong homonym, or documented structural misassignment retains its authoritative lexical display. |
| `NEEDS_REVIEW` | 0 | No unresolved citation decision remains. |

The previously unexplained group of 719 contains 547 source-supported Qal perfect 3ms headwords and 172 legitimate exceptions: 91 middle-weak ground forms with Qal support but no exact Qal perfect 3ms occurrence, 80 authoritative non-verbal headwords whose stable Strong identity also received a homonymous or misassigned verb row, and the bound preposition `l`/לְ whose single legacy verb row is a structural misassignment. Merely having a Qal occurrence is never treated as proof that the displayed form is Qal perfect 3ms.

Where OSHB does attest the Qal perfect 3ms of a middle-weak verb, the overlay now uses that exact occurrence form—for example בָּא, קָם, מֵת, שָׂם, שָׁב, מָל, and שָׁר—instead of the lexicon ground forms בּוֹא, קוּם, מוּת, שׂוּם, שׁוּב, מוּל, and שִׁיר. Forty-three stable IDs receive such an attested display override. These are extracted forms, not mechanically conjugated reconstructions. Where the exact form is absent, the approved headword remains unchanged.

The audit preserves a stable-ID-level record for all 1,603 identities under `hebrewVerbCitationAudit.records`, including displayed form, grammatical classification, exact provenance, independent-headword support, Lexical Index entry, corpus derivation, reconstruction status, and scholarly assessment. The verification-only Lexical Index is pinned in `data/metadata/open-scriptures-hebrew-lexicon-source.json`; raw lexicon content is not distributed. The older `hebrewVerbExceptions` list remains the narrow 398-identity no-Qal view for compatibility; the exhaustive four-way classification is authoritative for merge review.

## Hebrew morphology labels

`src/core/parser-core.js` now owns the complete learner-facing OSHB stem mapping. Core labels are Qal, Niphal, Piel, Pual, Hiphil, Hophal, and Hithpael. Supported additional Hebrew labels are Polel, Polal, Hithpolel, Poel, Poal, Palel, Pulal, Qal passive, Pilpel, Polpal, Hithpalpel, Nithpael, Pealal, Pilel, Hothpaal, Tiphil, Hishtaphel, Nithpalel, Nithpoel, and Hithpoel. Reader delegates to this mapping. A raw `N` is no longer a valid learner-facing substitute for Niphal.
